# Sand and Sanctuary: references (2026-11-12)

Research for Sand and Sanctuary (SCOPE §4.11). The brief is the user's lore; the materials behind it are real and
looked up here. Web searches only.

## The brief (user, 2026-11-12)

A region of the user's setting: **the Argent Sands.** Desert sand heavily mixed with dark silver oxide, littered with
the ruins of a fallen civilization. Themes: glass; fallen divinity in the pursuit of wisdom and answers; sphinxian
quixoticism; loss of self as an act of study. Bleak: it once aspired to be the great bright scholastic on the mount,
and is now a husk of itself. A dark theme, playing with silver tarnish and bright high points.

## The materials

- **Silver tarnish** (Canadian Conservation Institute): tarnish on silver is silver sulfide. Thin layers show
  interference colours that change with thickness; above about 100 nm the layer is black, silver sulfide's own
  colour. The thin early colours include yellow and blue (sources disagree on the full order; none lays out a
  chart). Silver oxide, the brief's sand, is a brown-black powder.
  *For us:* the ground is tarnish at its black end, warm brown-black in the lows. The interference colours stay out:
  their blue and violet sit on prepared and Pact Magic (§5.1), as Forge stopped before temper blue.
- **Tarnished silver's highlights:** on a worked silver object, tarnish gathers in the recesses and the raised parts
  stay bright, rubbed by hands and cloth. *For us:* frames dark in their recesses, their corners and top edges
  polished bright; the bright points few and sharp.
- **Desilvered mirrors** (mirror makers' and glaziers' notes): moisture reaches the silver behind the glass and it
  corrodes. It starts at the edges as dark spots and streaks and works its way in; it can't be cleaned off, being
  under the glass. Pre-1900 mirrors may be mercury-backed. *For us:* the portrait as a mirror that fogs as HP falls, rim and picture both, spots spreading in from the edges
  over the whole reflection (user, 2026-11-12): loss of self, drawn.
- **Fulgurites** (geology and museum notes): sand fused by lightning into tubes, glassy and bubble-pitted inside,
  rough with half-melted grains outside, sometimes branching like roots; tan, brown, black, white or translucent,
  by the soil. *For us:* glass as the scar of the fall; the crack of a hit branches as a fulgurite does.
- **Kintsugi in silver** (gintsugi): a break mended with metal, the seam left showing. *For us:* the heal moment,
  in silver, since gold is concentration's (§5.1).

## Desilvering up close (2026-11-12, for "closer to true")

Trade and collector guides (appraisers, glaziers, antique dealers), not conservation science; they agree on these:
- **Silver-backed mirrors** (after about 1835 to 1850; sources disagree): flat grey or black spots, flaking along the
  edges where damp got behind the frame.
- **Mercury (tin-amalgam) mirrors**, older: a soft, greyish, sparkly reflection; the loss grainy and sparkling, with
  speckled spots and cloudy patches.
- **Real against fake:** genuine foxing is soft, cloudy blooms with irregular edges and varied sizes, organic, often
  gathered at the edges; a perfectly repeated pattern of uniform dark dots is acid-antiqued modern glass.

*For us* (tools/sand-textures.mjs, `fog`): an edge band from each side, uneven, with wide soft fingers where damp
reached further, built of ten faint layers so it darkens gradually toward the frame, the corners (two sides
overlapping) darkest; blooms as wobbly outlines filled with radial gradients (a pale cloudy halo, a dark heart fading
to nothing), sizes spread wide; a few grains in each bloom, some catching light (the mercury sparkle, and the Argent
Sands' silver); a milky haze over all at low HP. The first draw, round radial dots, was the fake's look. Previewed alone
at 4× over a stand-in picture before going on the sheet.

## The palette (second pass, 2026-11-12)

Oxide sand (#17120e ground, #3a2b1f umber lows), ruin limestone (#d6ccb9 to #bdb19b, dark letters #1f1913), smoked
glass (the surface #1d1915 at 88% with a blur), pale desert glass (#e7dfbf, the open tab), silver (#eef0f4 bright,
#85837e tarnished edge). Silver alone read as one note (user, 2026-11-12); the four materials each take one place.
